Worth understanding first

What you're buying when you buy a lead.

Most lead companies run the same basic process: pull a list of homeowners in a zip code, text that list automatically, and sell the replies on as leads. In a lot of markets the same reply gets sold to more than one contractor.

None of that is a secret and none of it is complicated. The cost of running the process and the price of the output are very different numbers, and that gap is the entire case for running the system yourself instead of renting the result.

The questions that decide it

Common questions.

Do you book the appointments for me?
No. The AI works the conversation and qualifies the homeowner, then drops them into your pipeline. Your team calls them and books their own appointments. The booking conversation is where your crew's availability and judgement matter, so it stays with you.
Are the leads exclusive?
Yes. They're generated inside your own CRM from your own list. They go to nobody else, which is the part pay-per-lead can't offer — the same homeowner is routinely sold to several contractors in one market.
Why 1 lead per 600 texts when the live account did better?
Because a floor you beat is worth more than a forecast you miss. We'd rather you budget against a conservative number and be pleasantly surprised. Some markets outperform it, some underperform it — data quality, your offer and how fast you call people back all move it.
What's the real monthly cost, all in?
The system at $1,500–$2,500+, plus texting at about a penny a message. At 50,000 texts a month that's roughly $500 of texting on top. No setup fee, and your first 20,000 records are free.
How long before it's running?
Carrier registration is 3 to 10 days, the build is one to two weeks, and new numbers ramp their daily send volume gradually so the carriers trust them. The first couple of weeks are limited on purpose — that's what keeps your number alive long term.
